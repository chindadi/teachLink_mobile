# Privilege Revocation Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy defines a fair, documented process for removing or limiting a
project privilege. A privilege includes repository or organization access,
maintainer or other governance authority, permissions to manage project
resources, and participation rights granted by a project role. Revocation may
be temporary or permanent and may apply to only the privileges needed to address
the concern.

This policy does not replace the
[Code of Conduct Enforcement Policy](../COC_ENFORCEMENT.md). Conduct-related
restrictions must also follow that policy. Platform owners may separately
restrict access when required by law, platform rules, or an urgent security
response; they should document the reason and arrange a governance review as soon
as practicable.

## Grounds for Revocation

Privileges may be revoked or limited when there is a documented, proportionate
basis, including:

- a serious or repeated violation of project policy, role expectations, or a
  previously communicated condition of access;
- misuse of access, including unauthorized disclosure, alteration, or removal
  of project data or resources;
- a credible security risk, compromised account, or failure to follow a required
  security measure;
- sustained inactivity where the relevant role or access requires active
  participation, after following any applicable inactivity policy;
- a role ending, a requested transition, or a project need that makes the
  privilege unnecessary;
- a legal, platform, or operational requirement to remove access.

Revocation must not be used to punish good-faith criticism, disagreement, or
participation in governance. The decision-maker should consider the evidence,
impact, context, prior notice, and whether a narrower or time-limited measure
would address the concern.

## Initiation and Decision

Any maintainer, role owner, security officer, or person directly affected by the
privilege may request a review. A request should identify the privilege at issue,
the relevant facts and policy basis, and any supporting evidence. A request is
not itself a decision.

The maintainers decide governance-level revocations. The security officer may
recommend or take temporary access-protection measures within their documented
authority. Anyone involved in the matter or with a material conflict must
disclose it and take no part in the decision. Where practical, at least two
uninvolved maintainers review a non-emergency revocation; if that is not
possible, the decision and reason for the exception must be recorded.

Before a non-emergency decision, the affected person must receive notice of the
privilege under review, the reasons, and a reasonable opportunity to respond.
The written decision must state the action, scope, duration or conditions for
restoration, and appeal path. Access should be removed promptly after a final
decision, with only the minimum necessary access retained for an orderly handover.

If an immediate security, safety, or continuity risk requires action before
notice or full review, an authorized maintainer or security officer may impose a
temporary restriction. An uninvolved maintainer must review it as soon as
practicable, normally within 48 hours. The restriction is precautionary, not a
finding of wrongdoing. Notice should be provided when it can be done without
increasing risk.

## Appeal

The affected person may appeal within 14 days of receiving the decision notice.
The appeal should explain the grounds, such as material new evidence, a
procedural error, a conflict of interest, or a disproportionate outcome. It is
reviewed by uninvolved maintainers who did not make the original decision; if
none are available, the maintainers may appoint a neutral reviewer.

The reviewer may affirm, modify, or reverse the decision and should provide a
written outcome with reasons. An appeal does not automatically restore access
or pause a restriction. The reviewer may change interim measures where that is
consistent with security, safety, and a fair review. The appeal outcome is the
project's final internal review unless materially new evidence warrants reopening
the matter.

## Records and Review

The decision-maker keeps a restricted record of the request, evidence considered,
decision, and appeal outcome. Any public explanation must avoid exposing personal
or security-sensitive information. The maintainers own this policy and review it
annually and after a significant revocation. Amendments must follow the
governance change process and remain within the `Governance/` folder.
