# Conflict Resolution and Code of Conduct Appeals Process

## Purpose

This process defines how TeachLink Mobile resolves interpersonal and contribution
conflicts, and how participants may appeal a code of conduct decision. It provides
a clear, fair, and neutral path for disagreements of any size.

## Scope

This process applies to all participants in the TeachLink Mobile community:
contributors, maintainers, reviewers, and users. It covers disagreements about
contributions, decisions, and code of conduct enforcement.

## Conflict Resolution Steps

Participants first resolve disagreements directly and respectfully in writing. If
that does not work, a party opens a conflict issue describing the parties, the
topic, and a proposed resolution. The facilitator confirms the issue, gives each
party a chance to respond, and works toward agreement. The resolution is recorded
in the issue for transparency, and either party may return to this process for the
same matter only with new material information. Direct resolution remains private;
once escalated, the process is documented with personal details redacted.

## Neutral Facilitator

A neutral facilitator guides every escalated conflict. The facilitator is someone
with no substantial stake in the outcome — typically a maintainer who has no
involvement in the dispute, or an outside designee agreed by both parties. The
facilitator has no editorial stake in the contested contribution's outcome beyond a
fair process, and if no one can serve neutrally the maintainers appoint an external
facilitator.

## Escalation Path

If the facilitated process does not produce agreement, escalation proceeds as
follows:

1. The community lead reviews the record and attempts a final facilitated
   agreement.
2. If unresolved, the matter goes to the maintainers as a group; they decide by
   consensus, or by majority with a dissenting note.
3. Conduct matters that may result in enforcement action follow the code of conduct
   and its appeals process.

No party may escalate more than one level by forum-shopping; each level reviews the
recorded process and adds its decision.

## Code of Conduct Appeals

### Who May Appeal

Any person receiving a code of conduct enforcement decision may appeal: the
sanctioned party, the reporter of the incident, or a directly affected participant.
Appeals must be brought within 14 days of the decision notice.

### Appeal Steps

An appeal states the decision, the grounds (procedural error, new information, or a
disproportionate outcome), and the requested outcome. The appeals panel reviews the
record, may interview parties privately, and issues a written decision, typically
within 14 days. The decision may uphold, reduce, or increase a sanction and is
final barring exceptional new information.

### Independent Review

Every appeal is reviewed by a panel that did not participate in the original
decision, has no stake in the outcome, and is accountable to the conduct process.
Where possible, at least one reviewer sits outside the original enforcement team.
Panel members disclose conflicts before serving, and the record, excluding private
details, is summarized in the project's conduct log.

## Ownership

The community lead and the maintainers own this process and review it annually.
Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/` folder.

## Success

This process succeeds when conflicts are resolved fairly and without drama, and
appeals give anyone a meaningful, independent path to review a conduct decision.
