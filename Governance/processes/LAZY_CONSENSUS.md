# Lazy-Consensus Decision Process

## When It Applies

Lazy consensus is appropriate for routine, reversible decisions within an
already approved scope when no charter, role, or policy requires a formal vote.
It must not be used for maintainer appointments, charter amendments, permanent
privilege revocation, or any decision explicitly requiring a formal vote or
supermajority.

## Notice and Waiting Period

The proposal owner posts the proposed action, rationale, impact, decision owner,
and deadline in the relevant public issue, pull request, or discussion. The
minimum waiting period is 5 full calendar days from the notice. The owner may
extend the period when material new information is raised or affected
participants need more time.

## Objections and Outcome

Anyone may raise a reasoned objection before the deadline. A timely objection
blocks lazy consensus: the owner pauses the action, acknowledges the concern,
and works to resolve or narrow it. If unresolved, the question is revised,
deferred, or escalated to the appropriate decision process. Silence is not
treated as consent by an individual; it means only that no blocking objection
was posted during the notice period.

If no objection remains at the deadline, the decision owner may proceed and
records the outcome and any conditions. Lazy consensus never waives required
approvals, reviews, tests, or platform protections.

The maintainers own this process. Changes follow the governance change process.
